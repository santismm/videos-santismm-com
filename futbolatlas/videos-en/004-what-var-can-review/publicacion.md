## Sources and caveats

Everything comes from [Common VAR misconceptions](https://futbolatlas.app/k/var-misconceptions), [VAR](https://futbolatlas.app/k/var) and [VAR vs goal-line technology](https://futbolatlas.app/k/var-vs-goal-line-technology), which cite the VAR protocol in The IFAB's Laws of the Game 2026/27 (verified on the site on 8 August 2026).

| In the video | On the site |
|---|---|
| VAR only steps in for four kinds of decision: goal or no goal, penalty or no penalty, direct red card and mistaken identity | "VAR only exists for four match-changing categories — goals, penalty decisions, direct red cards and mistaken identity" |
| Mistaken identity: the referee books the wrong player | "mistaken identity when the referee cautions or sends off the wrong player of the offending team" |
| A foul in midfield that leads to nothing is not reviewable, however wrong the call | "A midfield foul that leads to nothing is not reviewable, however wrong" |
| It has to be a clear and obvious error | "only for a clear and obvious error or a serious missed incident" |
| Players can't ask for it; only the referee can start a review; the screen gesture risks a yellow card | "There is no team review request in the protocol. Only the referee can initiate a review… The TV-screen gesture from players changes nothing (and risks a caution for it)" |
| VAR doesn't decide: it recommends. The final decision always belongs to the referee | "The VAR recommends; the referee always takes the final decision" |
| A clearly incorrect second yellow can now be reviewed | "from 2026/27 a clearly incorrect second caution resulting in a red card is reviewable" |

- **Simplifications:**
  - The full threshold is "a clear and obvious error **or a serious missed incident**"; the video only names the first.
  - Mistaken identity covers both cautions and sendings-off given to the wrong player.
  - The video leaves out the 2026/27 competition option (immediately correcting a clearly wrong corner) and goal-line technology, which is a separate system.
  - "Goal or no goal" includes offences in the build-up (shown on screen, not spoken).
  - The "debatable / clear" scale in scene 4 is our own illustration of the threshold, not a chart from the protocol.
