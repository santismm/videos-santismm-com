- **Figures in the video, with the original wording:**
  - **11 months:** "our results of an 11 month Figure 02 robot deployment at BMW Group Plant Spartanburg" (Figure). On screen: "per Figure". BMW says "Within ten months".
  - **The task:** "sheet-metal loading, a classic pick-and-place task […] picks sheet-metal parts from racks or bins and places them on a welding fixture, after which six-axis industrial robots weld" (Figure). Three parts per cycle, "within a 5-millimeter tolerance".
  - **10-hour shifts:** "Ran 10-hour shift Monday-Friday" (Figure); "working ten-hour shifts daily from Monday to Friday" (BMW, 2026).
  - **1,250 h, 90,000 parts, 30,000 cars:** "90,000+ parts loaded", "1,250+ hours of runtime", "Contributed to the production of 30,000+ X3 vehicles" (Figure). BMW repeats them: "supported the production of more than 30,000 BMW X3 […] moved more than 90,000 components […] in around 1,250 operating hours" (2026).
  - **The three targets:** "Cycle time: […] The requirement was 84 seconds total, 37 seconds load time"; "Placement accuracy: […] Our target was > 99% success per shift"; "Interventions: Number of times a human must pause or reset the robot. The goal was zero per shift" (Figure). The post does **not** publish the values achieved; it only mentions "every intervention logged".
  - **The fleet:** in February 2025 Figure's founder wrote on LinkedIn "We currently have a fleet of robots performing end-to-end operations". BMW's spokesperson told Fortune there was "only a single Figure robot working in their South Carolina auto plant at any given time", "during non-production hours" until March, and later "a single robot performing a single task".
  - **What has not been published:** productivity against a worker or against the previous process, cost, return, or intervention rate. BMW says the pilot "demonstrated that Physical AI can deliver measurable added value", but gives no such measurement.
  - **Level B:** the figures come from the manufacturer (Figure) and the operator (BMW), which repeats them. [Methodology](https://physicalai.santismm.com/en/methodology): B is the manufacturer's or operator's own official publication, reliable about what it states but not independent verification.
- **More nuance that does not fit in 60 s:**
  - In 2024 BMW described a trial "lasting several weeks" and added: "Currently, there are no Figure AI robots at BMW Group Plant Spartanburg, and there is no definite timetable".
  - Figure says the forearm was its "top hardware failure point at BMW" and that it redesigned it for Figure 03.
  - Figure began retiring Figure 02 on 2025-11-19. On 2026-09-30 it said it melted most of the fleet at a foundry in Imatra (Finland) and that only a few units remain at its HQ.
  - A quick calculation the video does **not** make: 90,000 parts at 3 per cycle is about 30,000 loads, one per car. Spread over 1,250 h, that is about 150 s per load, against an 84 s target. But we do not know what those "runtime hours" include (tests, waiting, line stops). That is exactly the missing data, so the video leaves it out.
- **Animation:** the humanoid, rack, fixture and welding arm are diagrams, not a reproduction of the real work cell. The twelve robots in the "fleet" scene illustrate the word, not a stated number. The odometer is an analogy with illustrative numbers. The founder's quote is shown in its original English.
- **Thesis:** "Counting parts is not measuring results" is the video's editorial conclusion. It rests on the published figures measuring activity (hours, parts, cars) with no published comparison.

> ⚠️ **Discrepancy found on santismm.com:**
> - **The hours are not spread over 11 months.** The case page says "> 1,250 h over 11 months, 10-hour shifts Monday to Friday". Figure writes "Six months of daily runtime […] Across 1,250+ operational hours", and BMW "Within ten months". Also, 1,250 h on 10-hour shifts is about 125 shifts, roughly 25 working weeks. The 11 months are the whole deployment, ramp-up included. Suggested wording: "in about six months of daily use, within an 11-month deployment".
> - **Indirect source.** The case page cites the figures through Interesting Engineering (C). The primary source is Figure's 2025-11-19 post (B), and BMW confirmed the same figures on 2026-02-27 (B). The Figure 02 platform sheet cites both; the case page does not. The video cites the originals.
> - **Neither point changes the conclusion:** nobody independent verified the figures, and no productivity, cost or return has been published.
