## Sources and caveats

**Data:** METR results file ([benchmark_results_1_1.yaml](https://metr.org/assets/benchmark_results_1_1.yaml), linked from [metr.org/time-horizons](https://metr.org/time-horizons); the page was last updated on September 8, 2026).

**50% time horizon:**

| Model | Date | 50% horizon |
|---|---|---|
| GPT-2 | 2019 | 0.054 min (≈ 3 s) |
| GPT-4 | 2023 | 3.99 min |
| Claude 3.7 Sonnet | February 2025 | 60.4 min |
| Claude Opus 4.6 | February 2026 | **718.8 min ≈ 12.0 h** (95% CI: 316.7–3,633.8 min ≈ **5.3–60.6 h**) |

**80% horizon for Claude Opus 4.6:** 69.9 min.

**Doubling rate:**
- METR: "around every 7 months over the period 2019 to 2025" (TH1.1 blog post, January 2026).
- Since 2023, according to the data file: **128.7 days ≈ 4.2 months** (CI 104–158 days).

**Definition (arXiv 2503.14499):** "the time humans typically take to complete tasks that AI models can complete with 50% success rate".

**METR caveats reflected in the video:**
- They are "over a hundred diverse software tasks".
- The tasks are "much 'cleaner' than real economically valuable labor".

**"Today, twelve-hour ones":** this is METR's latest measurement for a released model (Claude Opus 4.6). METR also reports 17.4 h for "Claude Mythos Preview (early)", but warns that "measurements above 16 hrs are unreliable with our current task suite". That is why the video does not use that figure.

**Framing and thesis:** [The Stopwatch and the Exam](https://articles.santismm.com/the-stopwatch-and-the-exam/) ([Spanish version](https://articles.santismm.com/el-cronometro-y-el-examen/)): "The stopwatch is the right instrument and it is not a verdict."

> ⚠️ **Discrepancies found on santismm.com** (worth reviewing):
> - The benchmark tracker gives **14.5 h** for Claude Opus 4.6; METR's current file gives **12.0 h**.
> - The English article cites an interval of "about 6 to 98 hours"; METR's current interval for Opus 4.6 is **5.3–60.6 h**.
