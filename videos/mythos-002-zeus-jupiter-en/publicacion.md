"Jupiter is the Roman Zeus." Half true, and the nuance is useful well beyond mythology.

In the stories, they're the same: Ovid retells Zeus's myths under Latin names.
As an institution, they're not: Jupiter is the god of the Roman state, with Rome's first temple, titles born from battlefield vows and the promise of an "empire without end". Hesiod's Zeus rules because the gods asked him to, and guarantees no city.

The lesson: when two things "are the same", ask by which criterion. Story and function don't always match.
